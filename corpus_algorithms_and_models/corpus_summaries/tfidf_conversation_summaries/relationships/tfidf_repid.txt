The only thing that can heal a broken heart is time. 

Now, the real question is: How do you help facilitate the healing process? Well, you have to give your heart, and your spirit REHABILITATION THERAPY. 

Let's look at it medically: If you were in a car wreck and shattered your knee, you'd have surgery to **REPAIR** the damage, be in a cast for a few months to **PROTECT** the repairs, and then you'd need to exercise the tendons to **STRENGTHEN** the repairs. And those are the exact same steps you need to take with your heart. 

**REPAIR**: When you really and truly care for someone, when you really love them, you basically give them a piece of your heart. That fundamentally is what love is: giving someone a piece of you. The problem is that when they leave, for whatever reason, they don't give you that piece back. When someone takes your love and doesn't offer theirs in return, or stops giving their love, it leaves a hole, or tear, or a broken piece. And of course, the deeper, more connected the love, the more sever the damage.

What's the first thing they do to a gunshot victim before they start stitching them up? TAKE OUT THE BULLET! All those photos, cards, letters, texts, emails, videos, Facebook comments; those are like shrapnel in a broken heart. And as long as you keep them visible and accessible in your life, you're never going to heal. 

Now, let me clear, if they bought you an XBOX or a pair of shoes or outfit you really like, I'm not saying to toss it into a fireplace and light a match. I'm saying use you brain and understand what they left behind that you don't need to keep around. That stuff is called baggage. You have to let go of old baggage before you can begin thinking about picking up something new.

Some people turn to drugs or alcohol, some get into the gym and go crazy, some people shop, some turn to God. Well, I'm not going to argue the "right and wrong" ways, but IMO, the healthiest way is to fill that hole with love... from yourself. 

It's been my experience that whenever you get your heart broken, it typically leaves you in a vulnerable state. The worst heartbreak comes when you've given so much of yourself to someone else that you forget what it's like to just be "you". You were so wrapped up in "us" that "just me" is a forgotten concept. This is a perfect opportunity to have a love-affair with yourself. Find the "you" that you want to be. Make a list of all the things you want and need out of life, and start checking things off that list. It can be things you always wanted, things you never had the chance to do while in the relationship, things you lost when they left, or even a chance to just improve yourself. Go to a movie alone. Take yourself out to dinner. Go to the gym and push yourself to your limits, or take a class you've always thought about. Take a jog or stroll through a park on a sunny afternoon. Pick a famous spot in your town you've never been to and check it out. A museum or art exhibit, or a live show or concert. Find a new book to get lost in. Watch a television series you've always wondered about. Spend time with YOU. Reconnect with friends or family. Ask to babysit your cousins newborn. Take your kid brother/sister to the park or, hell, to Mcdonalds and let them play in the ballpit. Buy your mom/dad lunch. Do anything and everything you want to do, and do it just for you! 

Get back all those pieces of you, of your life, that got lost or taken away, and add a few more pieces to make you even better than before. The most important thing is to get to a point where your happy with who you are; that you really and truly LOVE yourself. After all, if you don't like/love the person you are, how do yo expect anyone else to?

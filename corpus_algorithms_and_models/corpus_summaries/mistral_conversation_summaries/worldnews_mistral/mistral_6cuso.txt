Ron Paul has proposed exempting tips from the Federal income tax.

A tip is a voluntary payment from a customer to a person who provided them with a service, to signify they appreciate the service.

It isn't part of the business's revenue, but a business may make accommodations for it in practice (eg. a tip line on credit card slips).

Servers who get tips may voluntarily agree on things like splitting the contents of a tip jar between them, or splitting tips with back-of-house staff like cooks.

A business can potentially implement a no-tipping policy (which some businesses do), or have rules regarding placement & signage on tip jars, so as to not interfere with serving.

But they can't tell servers what to do with their tips: it's the server's money, not the business's.

If the servers want to share with their supervisors, it is up to them.

If supervisors are getting left out of tipping (since they're not frontline staff, customers don't have reason to tip them directly), it's up to them to argue for an increase in wages to reflect this.

They might make $0.50 - $1.00/hr. more.

They're "coordinators" more than anything else.

The clean store, good coffee and the friendly and quick service is what's being tipped - the shift supervisors do just as much of that as the Baristas.

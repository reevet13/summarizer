There is a food crisis, via a distribution problem.

Global conglomerates are buying up consumable commodities and sitting on them creating this artificial "crisis."

There wouldn't be a food crisis if there wasn't a population crisis.

The way this is being portrayed in the media is doing exactly that though, mentioning how there are shortages of rice in some Costco's and so on, which is a distribution problem that is caused by people panicking and buying more rice than they normally bought.

The only thing the United States has to worry about with respect to food is not shortages but price increases.

People are not starving because there is no food, only because what food there is is too expensive.

Very clearly, with very small improvements to farming technique, all these smallholders / one acre farmers / subsistence agriculturalists (whatever you call them) can greatly increase their output, perhaps doubling it or tripling it, making them independent from the global food economy because they can grow more than enough to get by on their own.

The market is not always the answer.

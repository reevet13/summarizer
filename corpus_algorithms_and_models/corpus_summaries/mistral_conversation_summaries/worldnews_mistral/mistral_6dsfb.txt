Humans have the basic right to drink water, to search for water, to buy water.

A human right isn't something the government provides, its something you are born with -- the government can't take away.

Canada isn't saying people don't deserve fresh water and shouldn't be able to access it, just that water solutions have to be implemented within their own countries.

A right is something fundamental to humanity, but doesn't necessarily mean it's something a government has to "provide".

And there are few things more fundamental to staying alive than having water.

Making drinking water a basic human right is a very ugly grey area that is hard to enforce.

It implies that a technologically superior country is responsible for enforcing it since it is based on a physical technology that a poor country may not possess.

Water is a good. You don't have a right to a good/product or service because that means that you have a right to slavery.

I believe in equality though, in the sense that a human being born in the civilised world should not have more rights than someone born in a developing nation.

Water is a special resource that cannot be made into property. Some rivers flow through multiple countries.

We're going to have to learn to share the water, air and sunlight if we're ever going to get thru this.

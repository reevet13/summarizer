So, how are we to reconcile the disparity between those claiming to belong to a religion while at the same time saying they don’t believe in God?

I know plenty of people who went to catholic schools, grew up with catholic parents, but who don't actually believe in any of it.

They see "catholic" as a cultural identitifier, not a not a religious one.

In the UK nearly four in ten (38 per cent) people believed in a God, a further four in ten (40 per cent) believed in some sort of spirit or life force and two in ten (20 per cent) did not believe there is any sort of spirit, God or life force.

An atheist is not a member of some kind of secular cult or club, he/she is just someone who refuses to accept non-empirical  metaphysical claims, someone who refuses to make normative and substantive claims about superstitious belief.

Theism is not a *norm* from which we are obliged to indicate our having opted-out!

In Britain - at least in my experience - you are seen as a bit of a weirdo for being religious.

I'm suspicious too of the creeping use of the term "faith".

I feel that saying that people SHOULDN'T believe in God is just as offensive as saying that people SHOULD believe in a God.

Many people use Religion as a way to help strengthen themselves and it helps as a way for some people to build a basis of morality.

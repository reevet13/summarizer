This symbol is not called the swastika, but the manji.

It's been around since the inception of Hinduism.

The oldest commonly recognized use is from Ancient India, where it was indeed called the "Swastika" in Sanskrit.

There is nothing wrong with using the symbol if it's part of your religious tradition, as it is for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, etc.

In western society that symbol is strongly-- almost exclusively--associated with the atrocities of the Nazi party.

The swastika associated with Nazism is a right-facing swastika rotated 45 degrees, to resemble a square balancing on one corner.

The Buddhist and Hindu symbols can be left- or right-facing (signifying different gods and fortunes), and appear to be resting on their base.

It's manji in Japanese, and a lot of other things in different languages.

It's been around for thousands of years across dozens of cultures and languages.

The Nazis had it for 25 years.

As history goes, that's a minor deviation.

Getting on the watch list appears to be relatively easy. All Americans should try and get themselves on the watch list. As you get closer to having 1 in 1 American on the watch list, then it becomes useless. My friend got on that watch list just for having a last name that sounded like the war criminal Slobodan Milošević. Anybody can be on it, you don't even have to try. What seems useless to you and me seems like a great excuse to heavily monitor every single citizen to the government. It's not 1 in every 350 Americans, it's 1 million *people* from the entire planet Earth, regardless of country of residence. The vast majority of these are various Iraqis and Afghanis encountered by US troops in the field. Probably a bunch of Indonesians, Egyptians, Lebanese and Saudis as well. If the purpose of this Watch List is to alert authorities to impending catastrophe, then this is bad news. I think the sweet spot of watched Americans to all Americans has already been passed. There is obviously a point at which adding names to the list is by no means helpful to law enforcement. The perverse incentive is CYA. If somebody did some troublemaker type of thing in the past, and you the law-enforcement officer didn't add their name to the list, and then that troublemaker went on to be involved in something nasty, then you the law-enforcement officer get into trouble. There is no disincentive to add a name to the list.

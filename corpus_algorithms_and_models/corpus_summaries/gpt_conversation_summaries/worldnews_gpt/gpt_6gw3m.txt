I don't like the torture and such but I think Ashcroft could have a point. When they say it was forced through his mouth and nostrils, that seems to imply that they were more or less making the water go into the mouth with a hose perhaps. Whereas, putting a cloth over someones face and puring water on that cloth from some distance does not force it into it. I guess think about the difference between someone shoving a water hose in your mouth and someone pouring a bucket of water on to your face.

My guess is the only difference between today's version of waterboarding is the addition of the cloth, which seems as if it would make the sensation of being drowned much more intense.

There is a "possible distiction there" for certain.  In that much Mr Ashcroft does have "a point".

The problem comes that America's leaders are reduced to making tiny little distinctions about huge horrific torture.  

Very sad to be reduced to such silly word games when talking about such torture. 

It is as saying a tiger is less dangerous because he is missing a tooth.  But he will still rip you apart.

Waterboarding is not that difficult to try on yourself -- **with the help of friends for safety.**

Even when you're completely aware that you're not going to be permanently hurt, when your friends are standing by and there is no element of psychological torture involved, waterboarding is clearly torture.

It amounts to repeated drowning (not simulated drowning, *actual drowning*), just not to the point of death.  In other words, if performed correctly, you will get to experience a wracking, struggling drowning, over and over again.

Some people think drowning would be a pretty horrible way to die.  This is what we do to suspects, over and over again -- and for all they know, they are physically going to die each of those times, which makes it far worse.  It wouldn't be surprising at all if several years of that and solitary confinement broke the minds of suspects, literally driving them insane.

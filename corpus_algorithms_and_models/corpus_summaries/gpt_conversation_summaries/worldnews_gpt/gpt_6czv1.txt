The rage is there. Millions marched at the start of the Iraq War to protest it, and were ignored by the media. The question is, where has all the journalism gone? At least we still have Rage Against the Machine. Or do we? Listen to the so-called "alternative," "rap," and "rock" music nowadays and compare it to what came out in the 80s and 90s. Rage seems to have been replaced with egocentric consumerist blather. (1) No draft. Biggest difference. Doesn't affect most people personally, to the emotional degree that it did when they or their kids or brothers or whatever could be plucked out of US society and shoved into a stupid war at any given moment. Avoiding a draft is a way the US can and will continue to conduct wars whenever it feels like it. (2) Protesters have gotten goofy. Too many dudes with silly hats, die-ins, and street theatrics that preach only to the converted and look puerile to everyone else. (3) Package dealing other causes in with the anti-war effort. Some (not all) protests have speechifying and pontificating by communists, animal rights people, pro-choicers, and so on, which confuses the issue and makes it more difficult to support. (4) The vast center of the country doesn't react well to anger. It makes protesters seem scary. The most memorable protests in US history, at least (can't speak about other countries protesting this dumb and illegal war), had one of two features: (1) Immense dignity. (2) They were violent and destructive. The biggest factor? The lack of a draft. People are inherently selfish, and as long as they feel "the war has no effect on me" then you will see outrage only among the educated of society. True, but the government also exercises much more control over information about the war. We no longer watch villages being bombed, incendiaries being dropped on people, etc., on TV, because the government won't allow any images or information from the front lines to reach its citizens if it hasn't first passed through a government filter. It's hard to get outraged over stuff you don't know about, or which is presented as nondescript "collateral damage." The spark of rage has been struck over and over these past few years, but without the media seriously reporting about it, the spark cannot spread, so the fire dies out.

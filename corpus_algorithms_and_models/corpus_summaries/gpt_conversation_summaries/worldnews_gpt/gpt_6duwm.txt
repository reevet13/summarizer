The problem is that Venezuela has heavy crude and few refineries are able to process it. And these refineries are in the USA. The U.S. Gulf Coast is the largest recipient of Venezuelan crude oil imports, with refineries there specifically configured to handle Venezuelan heavy crude varieties. For all the bitching, the two countries seem to need each other. This is true. However, a portion of that oil is no longer being refined into American gasoline. If not replaced in a cost effective manner, and if more Venezuelan oil were diverted, the end result would likely be more expensive American gasoline. Actually, these facts are based on an aging reality. Most of the crude remaining on the planet is of the heavy, sour variety, including most of the oil in the Middle East. Refineries have been gearing up worldwide to process the heavy stuff. This is true of China, whose oil companies are now indicating that they are better prepared to process Venezuelan oil. It would be a very serious dispute that would result in the oil shipments from Venezuela to the US being halted, but there is little doubt that the Venezuelan government has been planning for the possibility. The question is how much heavy crude refining capacity has been built, and how long it will take to eliminate dependence on US refineries. Venezuela is a big exporter, so this would be a big shift in refining. The most likely scenario is the status quo.

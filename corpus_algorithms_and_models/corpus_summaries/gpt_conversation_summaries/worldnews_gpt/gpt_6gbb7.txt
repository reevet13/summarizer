When people buy cars, they tend to think of the other green stuff. Hybrids cost less to refuel.  
Diesel is a step sideways not forward. It might be better but it has its limits.  
But... diesels cost less to buy and get similar fuel economy improvements over regular gasoline engines.  
Hybrids are the wrong way to go. They more than double the complexity of the machine. Not only does the manufacturer jam a whole 2nd motor and batteries into an already-cramped small space, but the gasoline engine and the electric motor system have to talk intimately together as they trade drive responsibility back and forth.  
Compare this to an electric-only drive system, which is far *simpler* than a gasoline engine. It's basically just a motor, batteries, power controller, and charge controller.  
Hybrid tech in cars has much lower limits.

I'm not replacing my car until there's a decent plugin hybrid car out there. There's already diesel's in the UK that typically get 45-60mpg on the motorway (highway). 100mpg - infinite mpg is possible with a plugin hybrid.  
If you'd like something futuristic - [the Aptera has a 120 mile range, and goes 90mph (film)](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p2Ke1VWhZJA). And it's much cheaper than the Tesla.  
Hybrids are the right way to go if you choose the right kind of hybrid. The parallel hybrid is indeed more complex and a questionable road to take.  
With analog things like cars, adding more pieces tends to give you more pieces that can wear out and break down. More maintenance, more repairs (and at a higher cost, since you need more highly-skilled techs to deal with all the complexity), and more expenses.

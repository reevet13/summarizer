Of course Ellen should have the right to get married, just like anyone else. McCain said gays and lesbians should have the same legal rights to form unions as everyone. They shouldn't be given a 'right' to marriage because marriage is a religious sacrament. The deeper problem then, it seems to me, would be that he is therefore proposing that the government recognise marriages by *some religions* (which sanctify heterosexual marriage) but *not others* (which sanctify marriage of any two persons). What should happen is that marriage should receive no special sanctions from the state - instead _every_ couple could enter into a civil union and/or get married in a church of their choice, but benefits would only be given to those who joined a civil union. The handling of marriage in the law flies in the face of the separation of church and state. I'm of the personal opinion that the law should define the terms of civil unions, and ignore the concept of marriage entirely. That would remove the religious component and put all couples on equal footing in the eyes of the law. McCain doesn't support civil unions. He wants to leave the decision up to the states, and keep DoMA in place so out-of-state, same-sex marriages can be invalidated. In contrast, Obama supports repealing DoMA in his first term and establishing federally recognized civil unions, which extends the rights of marriage (though not the label) to all couples in the US. Regardless, both Obama and McCain think gays should not be allowed to get married. Period.

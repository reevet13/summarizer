Let it be said any society should provide safe drinking water, and I'll agree. It is immoral that many capable societies are so corrupt people have no safe drinking water. But, water isn't a "right". 

Canada isn't saying people don't deserve fresh water and shouldn't be able to access it, just that water solutions have to be implemented within their own countries. It's pretty much just a move to make sure there is no legal grounds for exporting Canadian resources without Canadian consent. 

In fact, since I'm being .us-specific, we already recognize "life" as a right [1]. And there are few things more fundamental to staying alive than having water. So it's a basic human right here already. 

Making drinking water a basic human right is a very ugly grey area that is hard to enforce. It implies that a technologically superior country is responsible for enforcing it since it is based on a physical technology that a poor country may not possess, whereas traditional basic human rights are abstract and can be enforced with good judgement and fairness without the need for technology. 

Water is a good. You don't have a right to a good/product or service because that means that you have a right to slavery (the service/work/goods of other people). This is why you should have the right to pursue those but not a right to be _given_ those. 

Water is a special resource that cannot be made into property. Some rivers flow through multiple countries.

In order to get the true gains/losses you'd have to subtract/add the net productivity gains/losses of the entire Dutch population as a direct result of drug usage. This is very difficult to do.

It's certainly true that using drugs can 'cost' you personally and drugs use also costs states a certain amount of money - but this is so whether you try to fight a war on drugs or not.

My argument is that fighting this war is a costly, pointless and often counterproductive business and that legalising all 'rereational' drugs would help to solve a lot of the world's problems without adding too many more problems.

If the dutch make that much money, imagine what the US could make.

If over the course of a year, these people smoke a pound of weed (a little over 1 ounce a month), and the US government charges $1,500 for this pound (mid-range estimate), then the government would make 75 BILLION DOLLARS.

if we're talking money then putting people in prison rakes in billions of dollars for the private corporations who run them (and for their shareholders)

Fact is, regardless of financial incentives, these drugs should be legal.

The US population is about 18.17 times that of the Dutch population... all other things remaining equal, this would mean about 5.452 billion pounds in tax revenue... which would equal a tax revenue of somewhere around 11 billion dollars.

Add that to lower infrastructure costs from not having to house so many prisoners, and the actual gain to the US is probably around 20 billion... or 2/7ths of what Iraq costs us every year.

Even so, there would be an extraordinary reduction in the tax burden, just from not spending the billions and billions of dollars that currently just destroys lives, fills prisons, corrupts governments and agencies, funds organised crime, and wastes law enforcement resources.

Most of the damage to individuals ascribed to drugs is actually caused by their prohibition.

People just can't think rationally when it comes to drugs. Its ironic that while drug use may be unhealthy, society's knee jerk response to it is even more harmful to society as well as the users.

I still think legalisation is better than what we do now. You can't wish away the bad stuff: you have to deal with it.

I am always amazed that people don't realize that boycotts breed counterboycotts.  
I'm guessing this will have the same amazing effect boycotts usually have, which is none. Abstaining from buying some stuff and then buying it later on some other day has always seemed like a retarded way to "send a message".  
Who knows how much traction this boycott will get, but Carrefour is one of the biggest store chains in China. Their stores are usually very busy, and there are lots of them. They very often have the best selection of mid-quality household goods of any of their nearby competitors.  
If the boycott really happens, it may have a significant effect on Carrefour's bottom line.  
However -- and I'm saying this as someone who is not opposed on general principle to China ruling Tibet -- I think the people organizing this are hypocritical. Essentially, they're saying, "We're going to demonstrate against the people demonstrating against us, which will show them their demonstrations have only hardened our resolve!" And the thought that the other side will have exactly the same reaction somehow never occurs.  
Not by any means a uniquely Chinese flaw in thinking, but it still makes me laugh.  
No matter how bad a country's government is, the citizens still feel a certain amount of patriotism towards their country. Boycotting the Olympics is not seen as a move against the government, but as an affront to the Chinese people (from the viewpoint of Chinese).  
Therefore, despite the political ramifications of human rights and so forth, the Chinese people are pretty disgusted over the actions in the rest of the world, and that's why there's so much support for the nation, and by extension, the government of China.  
Like most boycotts and protests, the goal is probably more to vent their anger/frustration/annoyance than to accomplish anything concrete.

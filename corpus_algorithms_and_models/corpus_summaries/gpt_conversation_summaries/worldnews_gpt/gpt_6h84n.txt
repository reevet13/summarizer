There are horses that are hauled to Canada and Mexico for slaughter. A lot of them. But I still support a no slaughter policy, because as someone who was active in the horse trade, I know that it wasn't the old horses or the crippled horses that mostly went for slaughter. Most of the horses that went for slaughter did so - because they were far more valuable by the pound than they were as back yard horses. Often horses that were crippled had been dosed with medicines that made the meat not safe for human consumption for long periods of time - and I recall numerous occasions where the plants had shut down due to the safety of the meat for being eaten, and often because a horse had been dosed with something shortly prior to it's death, and made someone in the world sick. I would guess that only one tenth of the horses previously shipped from this territory are still going for slaughter. Here, most of the meat shippers ship to Canada, despite Mexico being closer. The whole thing where they are saying that crippled horses go for slaughter is for the most part myth, though. I still dabble in horse rescue and an equivalent of horse hospice. I buy starved out horses, and fatten them up, but I no longer sell them. Mainly because I feel that after a lifetime of service, we owe them as painless a death as possible. Swiss, at least in the French-speaking part, are huge fans of horse meat. Many Americans seem disturbed by this when I mention it.

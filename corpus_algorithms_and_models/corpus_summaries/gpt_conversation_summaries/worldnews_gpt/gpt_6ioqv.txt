A good deal of the news, however, is also absent from “The Daily Show.” In 2007, for example, major events such as the tragic Minneapolis bridge collapse were never discussed. And the shootings at Virginia Tech, the most covered story within a given week in 2007 by the overall press, received only a cursory mention.

The Daily Show is an excellent program, for sure. The main news outlets try to grab viewers by making flashy graphics and dumbing down the news to a level that Joe Sixpack can find as a compelling narrative. The Daily Show grabs viewers by being funny, but the subject matters are usually deeper than that of the MSM. Jon Stewart and Stephen Colbert shouldn't be confused with Edward R. Murrow, but they're definitely the Jonathon Swifts of our generation. Both have their own brands of satire, and both are very effective.

A year or so back there was a study saying the Daily Show contained as much substantive news coverage as any network news show. Stewart was quoted saying, "That's impossible, you can't become informed watching my show."

Satire, news, comedy... whatever. It's presented in a very effective manner. I think a lot of people appreciate it when someone actually makes intelligent points every now and then and most of the news that comes out is so ridiculous it deserves to be satirized anyway.

The Daily show reminds me of "one of Shakespeare's fools. Wise in everything but name, they were the only ones who dared to tell the king the truth."...

Why is anyone surprised? Satire has always been about reveling the truth by mocking popular lies.

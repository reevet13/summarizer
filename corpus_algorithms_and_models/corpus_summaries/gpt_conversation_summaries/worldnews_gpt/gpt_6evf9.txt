People are not concerned with developments which do not threaten their immediate comfort or safety. In order to permeate the public consciousness it is important to present horror stories of what has gone wrong in the passed and how this may lead to problems for them in the future. It is too bad this is such a shrill and poorly written article, because the use of genetic data by governments and corporations is an important issue. They take each baby's blood to test for rare diseases. However, that means that they have a card with each child's identifying information and a sample of his/her blood. The point here is, we will never actually know what they do with that DNA. We are losing our freedoms and rights as an individual... a little at a time. Not to mention the possibilities of selective biological weapons that only target a certain 'group' of the population. Maybe nothing, but if anything is sure about a huge government database filled with juicy details, it is that it will be hacked in short order, compromising the information you entrusted your government with. I agree that this article was hysterical &amp; reactionary, but I think this issue does warrant trepidation. I would definitely be okay with allowing the DNA samples to be taken *anonymously* and used toward medical research, but I would not want names associated with the samples. I agree, it warrants *thought*.  This article, however, provided none.

Cluster bombs rule

Why the hell would the US military use cluster bombs in a city? It doesn't make any military sense. In this day and age any air-to-ground engagements are done using guided munitions. The military wants to make ABSOLUTELY SURE they kill the people they want to kill. Not drop a non deterministic weapon like CBs. CBs are good for infantry or equipment but suck in a tight environment like a city.

The argument is basically that as cluster bombs and the sub munitions they distribute are indiscriminate in what they kill, unlike say a conventional missile or bomb.

Therefore their use in certain conditions (i.e. in a built up area that has a lot of civilians / civilian infrastructure) may mean that their use contravenes the Geneva Conventions as it is incompatible with the obligation to distinguish between civilians and combatants.

Bombing cities is generally prohibited, you can attack targets within cities, but you are supposed to do so whilst minimising both civilian casualties and damage to civilian infrastructure.

If you are bombing a target within a city you are supposed to try and ensure that all you are hitting is the target (although collateral damage does occur you are supposed to try to mitigate it), cluster bombs, unlike most other munitions, are not easily employable in this scenario hence the argument I posted before.

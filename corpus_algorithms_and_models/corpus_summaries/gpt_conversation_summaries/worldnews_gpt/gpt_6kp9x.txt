With that said, I do live on a farm in a rural area of my state. The only thing that would give me a problem is my electric powered well. Think about it, a bunch of Indians three millennia ago correctly predicted the a year, when peak oil, nuclear weapons or a technological singularity threaten to destroy the world. The world has never before been in such danger of destruction. However, I'm not convinced this is necessary at all, it could be paranoia. Hers does seem an extreme example, but it never hurts to be prepared. My friends and I half-seriously make plans for the coming zombie apocalypse. It's important to keep a sense of balance. I've always enjoyed survivalist literature for the DIY aspect, but the paranoia is worthless as far as I'm concerned. Take this woman for instance. She cut up her credit cards and started a garden and ended up losing weight. She even built a cord wood home. How awesome. But those kinds of great lifestyle choices don't have to inspired by paranoia. You can do those things just because you think it's cool and fun. I see nothing to fear in the future. The news is so focused on doom and gloom and yet this whole equities bubble is such a blatantly obvious confidence trick. The world is a complex place. Some societies could possibly fall, but others could benefit. A total worldwide collapse seems highly highly unlikely given regional variation in energy supplies and production mechanisms.

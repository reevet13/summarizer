some of my friends used to work at boston market, subway, etc. and they weren't allowed to give away food that they were about to throw away. So after they threw it away the homeless people would go into the trash can and get food from there.

Some towns and cities ban restaurants from giving away food to the homeless.  The thought is that if the restaurants feed the homeless, then more homeless will be attracted to the town.  And no town wants to have a large homeless population.  So, they ban helping homeless people.   

&gt;   And consider this: the rotting food that ends up in landfills produces methane, a major source of greenhouse gases.

Supermarkets should have ongoing deals with food banks so that they receive the dated and blemished food. Hold the supermarkets harmless for liability and setup a storage area for the food banks to access so the food doesn't go to waste.

Many markets and restaurants already do, but only in states that protect them from lawsuits.

The concern is they could donate perfectly good food to a pantry/soup kitchen, but through improper handling by these groups, it causes food poisoning, and through no fault of their own, the originating restaurant/market would get sued.

Some states have passed 'good Samaritan' laws wrt food donation, which reduces potential legal exposure and sets a bar on responsibility for donaters.

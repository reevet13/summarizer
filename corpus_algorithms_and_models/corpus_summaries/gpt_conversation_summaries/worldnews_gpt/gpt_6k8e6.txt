I think you are mixing up Japanese policemen with law enforcement from another asian country. While I can provide many citations about the lack of police brutality in Japan, there is virtually nothing comparable to the aggressive nature of officers in the US.

The riot police are committed to using disciplined, nonlethal force and carry no firearms. They are trained to take pride in their poise under stress. Demonstrators also are usually restrained. Police brutality is rarely an issue. When excesses occur, the perpetrator is disciplined and sometimes transferred from the force if considered unable to keep his temper.

In order for a department to get funds from the government, they must solve crimes...or at least be seen to be solving crimes. The better a department's statistics, the more money they recieve. It behooves them to get a confession. Forced confession is quite common in Japan in fact. Especially if you are a foriegner.

Confession (jihaku) equals conviction in Japan. Full stop. It will NOT be overturned later in court.

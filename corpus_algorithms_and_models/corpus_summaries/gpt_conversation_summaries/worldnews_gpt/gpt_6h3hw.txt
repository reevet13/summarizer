Prime Minister Fukuda Yasuo said, “I will not be affected by the court decision.” 

This shouldn't exactly come as a surprise, given that [Article 9 of the Japanese Constitution](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_9_of_the_Japanese_Constitution) states:

&gt; ARTICLE 9. Aspiring sincerely to an international peace based on justice and order, the Japanese people forever renounce war as a sovereign right of the nation and the threat or use of force as means of settling international disputes. (2) In order to accomplish the aim of the preceding paragraph, land, sea, and air forces, as well as other war potential, will never be maintained. The right of belligerency of the state will not be recognized.

It is very interesting, since the SDF, at one point, was ruled unconstitutional as well - and there is a lot of debate whether the judiciary are lapdogs of the government or not.

As far as I can tell, the reason for three branches of government: executive, legislative and judicial, is so that there are adequate checks and balances for the proper interpretation of the constitution, or the main document of how the three branches are supposed to interact and make and enforce laws.

In other words: it's unconstitutional (and illegal) for Prime Minister Fukuda Yasuo to say "I will not be affected by the court decision." He must bow to the judiciary and end the unconstitutional practise by the JSDF or he must rewrite the law that makes the JSDFs actions illegal (in this case amend the constitution.)

The Japan constitution is a beautiful document.

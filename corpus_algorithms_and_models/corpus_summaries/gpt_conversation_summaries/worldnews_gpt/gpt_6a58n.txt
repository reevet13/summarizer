The problem is that the generalities are generalities instead of the exceptions. The so called "captians of industry" lobby the curriculum designers to teach in such a way that the students only really learn how to show up on time and do as they are told. Part of the problem we face is the teaching of history from books decided by committees who don't research the books, but are bought off by the book companies. A lot of the "facts" in the history books are legends and myths presented as fact, kind of like watching FOX news. 

No child left behind did not create this problem, and the Democrats will not solve it. The schools aren't failing so much as our society at large is failing on this one. Literature contains our cultural heritage. You can learn a lot about a time just by reading books written then. Without understanding the general culture of a time and region it is difficult to attack the more complex works of that time and region. Reading books from a given time also allows you to absorb history in a much richer way. 

The question we should be asking is not "are our children learning" but instead "what are our children learning". Ignorant people are easier to manage was the gist of my comment. I think that forcing kids to read literature destroys the love of it. The mindset matters more than the material. Schools can't do this, because they can't numerically measure it.

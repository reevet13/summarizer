As the saying goes, even in the West, we are only ever three meals away from revolution. When I see people wasting food, it really makes me sick. With all our technological advances, we seem to be sliding backwards. We in North America are so used to cheap (we spend less than 10% of our income on our greatest need) endless (and hardly nutritious) food that we take for granted its availability. Even in Thailand, which produces 10 million more tons of rice than it consumes and is the world’s largest rice exporter, supermarkets have placed signs limiting the amount of rice shoppers are allowed to purchase. Not to mention that things are actually better than they used to be in the dark ages where most people were starving except the oligarchs. If most of us simply decide not to eat meat for one week, or brown-bag our lunches, and donate the savings to the World Food Program, the impact would be tremendous. That's my plan for the rest of April and May. Not much but something for starters. I question the corrolation some of the comments here are drawing between pitching leftovers after a meal on the town and families in India not being able to afford rice or cooking oil. It may be an illustration of that inequity, but it's certainly not the prime factor in it. If the UN's goals and policies provide *temporary* food relief while encouraging long-term agricultural production, then it will help. If it just dumps food on them and devastates their farmers, it will hurt.

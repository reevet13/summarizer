This article was published on Thursday July 17 2003. Not that this invalidates the message, but just for those more attached to the "news" stuff.

This has become a lot less relevant since the article was written. Blair earned a lot of hatred for his blind following of the US and the public no longer see many benefits of the "special relationship" we have.

Bush won back to back elections and now he is the most unpopular president in the last 100 years. Blair won two elections convincingly before 2003, in 1997 and 2001 (held before September 11th, in June). In 2005 Labour lost most of its huge majority, largely due to Blair and Iraq. They would have lost the election directly if the British electorate would have had a viable alternative, as the Spanish did in Spain when they ousted Aznar, instead of the weak Tories. From 2005 on, the ongoing electoral setbacks for Labour can be attributed to Blair fatigue, despite the fact that he was pushed into retirement by Brown and his supporters. His last years in office were rife with scandal, controversy and general malaise, much of which stemmed from his "special" relationship with the US. His popularity, which was once as great as that of Bush, is now dismal, and hatred for him has grown. In the eyes of many, he is a disgraced politician.

In the latest elections Labour got 24% of the national vote, the worst for 40 years. But in the local elections following the invasion of Iraq, when Blair was in power, Labour got 26% of the national vote. He endured massive criticism for his following of the US and this ended the days of him being incredibly popular.

Still, Britain is nowhere near as independent or powerful as we all like to believe. But I don't see how that is surprising. Furthermore, have you ever seen Brussels treat us fairly? If we were aligned closer to Europe we would be similarly bastardised just to a different master.

The British sold their sovereignty to the US out of desperation during WWII. Unable to pay for the American weapons sold to them, they agreed to things such as the US Lend-lease Act, where the US "lent" them tanks, guns, and munitions, in exchange for military bases in Britain and other similar forms of payment. Thus, the US didnt forcefully take their sovereignty, the British just gave it away so that they wouldn't become part of Germany during WWII.

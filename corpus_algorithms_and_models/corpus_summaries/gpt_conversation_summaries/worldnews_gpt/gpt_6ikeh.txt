In 70AD the Romans destroyed the Second Temple and ended Jewish self-rule in Israel.  They then renamed the area "Filistina" after the Philistine people who lived to the north of Israel, and who had been the enemies of the Jews.

A few hundred years later, after the birth of the Islamic-Arab empire, the Arabs invaded and captured the entire Levant (Israel, Jordan, Lebanon) from the Byzantine empire.  They held it until the Turks took over.  The Turks held it until World War 1.  Then the British held it and promised the entire "Palestine" area (ie: Modern Israel + Modern Palestine + Modern Jordan) to the Jews.  Then they promised it all to the Arabs.

So after World War 2, the U.N. decided to give East of the Jordan River to the Hashemite Arab kings (kingdom of Jordan), then split the remaining region between Jews and Arabs.  The Jews accepted this partition plan, and the Arabs rejected it, in 1948.  Thus were born modern Israel and modern Palestine.

The Jews never wanted to leave Israel, they always prayed to go back, and numerous attempts I didn't relate to you (because they failed) were made by the Jews to reestablish sovereignty in Israel.  In 1948, they finally succeeded, but by then the Arabs had lived there for so long they felt entitled to keep their ill-gotten lands.

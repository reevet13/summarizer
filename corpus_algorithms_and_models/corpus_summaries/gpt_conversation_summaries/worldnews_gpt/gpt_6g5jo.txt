I left them a while ago and not only am I getting the download speed I pay for now, I don't have to worry about torrent downloading or, which is even worse, having websites load slower because the owner doesn't pay Virgin for the "honour" of connecting a user to their site.  
Also, the government is stupid in letting them monopolise this.  
Virgin is the company that owns the fibre-optics.  
Every other company either leases the lines or uses the BT lines (like my own provider) which are obviously far slower and on top of that, do not have the capacity for higher bandwidths that fibre-optic cables do.  
This may seem like a non-issue currently when "up to" 8mb suits many customers but there will come a time when this is insufficient.  
If you don't like how your ISP treats your traffic, get a new one.  
If you don't care about net neutrality, maybe you'll be able to get cheaper service.  
Everyone will win and possible advances that violate "net neutrality" (because the term IS bollocks) won't get quashed by government intervention.  
I have no love for ISPs who abuse their power, but I think they should be dealt with like any other business; by empowering the consumers to vote with their dollar.  
Many people are concerned that unscrupulous ISPs will gain monopolies over particular transport mechanisms, and thereby force people into their traffic-biased network.  
This is a valid concern, but ultimately, I think consumer choice will win out.  
The number of choices last mile content delivery has multiplied in the last few decades.  
As such, there is much room for competition.

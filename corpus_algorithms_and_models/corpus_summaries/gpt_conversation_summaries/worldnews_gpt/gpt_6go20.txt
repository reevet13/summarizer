"Several employee bulletins dug up by the FAA show that Continental management was concerned by the number of fuel stops pilots were making on international flights into Newark, at one point going so far as to connect the costs of unnecessary refueling with profit sharing and pension funding."

They never fill the tanks completely. Each flight has just enough fuel to get there plus a safety margin. There is no sense in wasting more fuel to carry the weight of unneeded fuel.

FAA found all of them in compliance with this rule -- on average they had 64 minutes of fuel remaining.

You don't have to fill to the top of the tank. The point is just that the safety regs were never meant to be followed this close to the letter.

Well, the point of using the 757's is to say, "Look, we just flew 3300 miles. This plane's max range is 3900. We need to get on the ground." By using a plane that approaches its limits in range, they have a more plausible excuse for getting on the ground faster and using less fuel.

"Minimum fuel" doesn't necessarily get you on the ground faster (only "emergency fuel" will do that.) And both are investigated and tracked by the FAA.

There are times where headwinds make the flights impossible. That requires an expensive and time-consuming mid-flight landing in a place like Gander or Bangor. That is incentive enough for Continental to put new aircraft on that route (which are on-order, deliveries should start in 2009.)

You've loosely described what just happened in the sub-prime market. Many people were trading in lightly-traded contracts (thus difficult-to-value contracts) with models that didn't take into account the potential for the entire credit market to seize. Thus, when the first few people had to sell at lower-than-book prices, everybody else had to mark their assets down, leading to margin calls, more selling, lower prices, etc etc.

I'm not an economics expert, but I suspect that if the oil price suddenly unwinds then it will affect the credit markets just as the sub-prime crash did. A few banks may be allowed to fail, and everybody will become even more aware of counterparty risk.

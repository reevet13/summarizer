The woman was involved in a non work-related car accident and suffered injuries. She was not immediately able to recoup medical costs from the driver at fault, so her health plan stepped in and paid them. Under her agreement with the health plan, they provide this payment with the understanding that you will pay it back to them if you later win damages covering your health expenses.

She sued and eventually settled with the trucking company for $700,000, and now the Walmart health plan wants the money back which they fronted to cover her expenses, as provisioned in her contract.

While it's upsetting to hear that she was injured and Walmart is trying to take money away from her, she in fact received two payments for the same thing. The first was coverage from her health plan, and the second was her settlement with the trucking company who injured her. If she's now in a situation where she doesn't have enough money, that's a problem created by the combination of high health care costs and a stingy settlement from the trucking company. Walmart's stance is simply that they won't be paying for someone else's negligence now that the liability has been legally established in court.

The only court Wal-Mart is losing in is the court of public opinion. Everyone anti-walmart loves to harp on "they make enough money, let's set precedent for invalidating contracts!"

The $400K left was after legal expenses, but before Walmart's collection. She now has nothing left.

Walmart claims to cover their employees health care. They should cover her health care. No one's arguing for her right to leave money to her children after her death, but "permanently brain damaged" sort of suggests that her health care isn't over yet.

San Marino, with a population of about 30,000, is at the end of the long list of 218 countries compiled by the center. It has a single prisoner. The US is a strange one. I write a friend in a New York prison. He's into his eighth year of imprisonment for a crime that would net him no more than a year in Canada. I can't believe how harsh US penalties are. Prisons are big business, not only for the contractors that build and staff them, but also for the companies that profit from prison labor. Zero tolerance drug laws need to be abolished. The bewildered tone of this article is amusing: Criminologists and legal scholars in other industrialized nations say they are mystified and appalled by the number and length of American prison sentences. The article contains nary a mention of the for-profit prison industry, or the effects of lobbying on sentencing laws. Its weak conclusion is that tough crime laws are attributable to... democracy. The United States, in fact, has relatively low rates of nonviolent crime. It has lower burglary and robbery rates than Australia, Canada and England. Correctional institutions should never have been made private, profitable. In the U.S. prison is used as a means to punish a person. In Europe prison is used to rehabilitate. In the U.S. when prisoners come out, they are much more likely to be more violent, more hateful and better criminals with little alternative but to continue a life of crime.

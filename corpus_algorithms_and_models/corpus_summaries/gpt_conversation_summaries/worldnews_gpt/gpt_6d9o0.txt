As a side note though, the goverment did subsidize the bread, thus making it a lot cheaper than market price. I'm no economist but isnt this something that was bound to happen when commodity prices surge?

there is a reason for the subsidies and this is to pay off poor people to not revolt. also in 1977 when the egyptian government did cut subsidies on staple foods and natural gas, the country erupted in riots that nearly overthrew the Sadat government. After that the govt has been very wary of doing anything similar.

I live in Egypt, and living conditions here are getting worse by the day. There were recent killings at some areas over who gets to buy the bread first. And I mean very low quality, barely edible bread. At some days I pass by a bakery where they have very cheap bread, and there is over 50 persons waiting in line for hours to get a few cents worth of bread. All necessary food items have risen in price by more than 50% (sometimes 100%) in the past year and people's salaries remain the same. The government is doing absolutely nothing to fix this, and I think that in a few years, people will no longer withstand it and something big will happen. The poor are actually fighting (and recently, dying) for bread and basic life necessities. A meal that contains meat is considered a dream by many. A *lot* of people save money to be able to eat some meat every now and then. We have a general strike on the 6th of April.

Not about the wheat. The strike is against the failure of the government to do anything about the ridiculous rise in prices of basic life necessities. The government seems to be busy arresting political opponents while the living conditions are becoming unbearable for a segment of the people that were once living fine.

Problem is that Egypt imports nearly most of its wheat (in fact Egypt is a net food importer), that's why it's so vulnerable to international surges in price.

Egypt used to be a net food exporter but was in part forced by the World Bank to switch to growing mainly cotton for export (export led raw material dogma of development technocrats). Of course the landlords that own most of the land were only too happy to do this as it nets them loads of money.

Well, I am not sure so much what they are making, but the US has the world's largest manufacturing base at 1725 billion USD in 2006 (for comparison, China is #2 at 1096 and Japan is #3 at 929)

Using the same figures used in the article you reference, you can project (not in a fool-proof manner obviously, but good enough for this type of speculation) the trend shown , into the future somewhat (into the present too since the data is only good up until 2006). Doing so (in a pessimistic manner regarding China and optimistic manner regarding the US) shows that China will have a larger manufacturing base in 2009 and exceed the US's total export's in 2007 (the US and China are almost at parity in terms of exports in the data shown with only an 8 billion USD difference in 2006, which is well within the margin of error for this data anyway).

So whilst the US is ahead in 2006 it may well not be now, and certainly wont be in the future unless the US starts to focus on manufacturing (which it has been de-emphasising) and exports (which may increase as the dollar drops).

What you will need to watch out for is who is getting the benefit of all that economic activity (cue globalisation and international organisations owning stuff) and also to what extent US infrastructure and US companies come under foreign control or foreign ownership (after all there are lots of people holding a devaluing dollar and there is a recession on, might be a good time to go shopping in the US!).

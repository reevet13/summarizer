If supply and demand are matched up well than we expect neither shortages, or dramatic surpluses. Many people have argued that there is no oil supply problem by pointing at the lack of shortages. This is faulty logic. It just shows that the price of oil is not so low as to result in a shortage. 

We are consuming all the oil we can pump and are willing to pay the extra cost for it. 

A large influx of investor dollars chasing profits in a "can't-crash-nothing-but-up" market are the fuel on the fire of bubbles. 

As long as the crude is delivered and there is no surplus or shortage then the price is right. 

The problem with this speculation theory is that you can't create real speculative bubble (where price is more than the value of the stuff) with deliverable commodity like oil without storage capacity. Oil storage capacity is minimal compared to oil delivery volumes. All storage in the world equals to 3-4 days supply. 

Speculation works because oil is worth much more than $120/barrel and speculators are right. It has been kept down mainly for political reasons. Price goes up but consumption is not going down. When we reach state where price increase leads decrease in consumption, the price is right.

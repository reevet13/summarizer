It's more complex. Food demand is very inelastic.

Of course global food shortages are linked to biofuel use - everything on earth is somehow linked. But that does not mean that biofuels are responsible for the current rises in food prices. Not yet.

1 - Climate events (unusual harmful weather) severely reduced grain harvests in Australia and North America. (This has caused stored grain reserves to be drawn down dangerously low. Good harvests in Australia and North America this fall would greatly reduce the problems. But, if harvests are poor in either or both 2 years in a row... I don't even want to think about the horror of it.

2 - Rice and grain consumption in China, India and much of the 3rd world has steadily increased for years, and the rate of increase is now accelerating far faster than agricultural productivity. This pressure will not only continue but increase for the foreseeable future.

3 - Unstable oil market (Iraq) simultaneous with greatly increased demand, especially from growing India and China, increased oil prices, which increased the price of everything from fuel for production and transport of food to chemical fertilizers.

4 - Diversion of previously food producing lands into inefficient but profitable biofuels reduced food supply. But it is too soon for the major impact of that diversion to be felt yet. Just wait, it'll get a lot worse.

While it is certainly true that bio-fuel is causing an increase in commodity prices don"t forget about the exploding economies in India, China and Russia where their populations are HUGE. As their economies improve the populations there eat more western style diets. At this time they do not have the infrastructure to grow their own and that is why they are importing more from the US and South America.

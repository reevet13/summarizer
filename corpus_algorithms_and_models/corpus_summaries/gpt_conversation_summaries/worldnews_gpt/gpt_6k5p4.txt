There are a lot of sides to this issue. Although I have family in the Netherlands (near Delft), I can't speak for the Dutch as a whole, let alone those from Amsterdam, but my understanding is this...

What would it be like to be from one of the most progressive cities (and countries) in the world? Where prostitutes are regulated, where soft drugs are tolerated, where doctors are sometimes permitted (that is, not prosecuted) to perform euthanasia?

The Dutch have taken a very pragmatic view towards drug use, prostitution, and many other societal issues, but that does not mean that they like them. Rather, they understand that even when these things are outlawed, they still occur anyway. Many Dutch remain very strictly religious, in accordance with their history as one of the hotbeds of Calvinism (and other forms of Protestantism).

So, the view of the Dutch government for much of the last four hundred years has been to regulate and control vices. But this has seesawed back and forth many times. Cannabis use is still technically illegal, existing in a shadowy realm of uncertainty. See also the above-mentioned euthanasia controversy. Their government (like any government) is always moving back and forth, left and right.

Recreational drugs and commercial sex are legal in Amsterdam. Unfortunately the world (mostly egged on by the US) through FATF have identified both as money-laundering related activities effectively preventing legitimate financial businesses from having anything to do with them at risk of facing sanctions. So it means if you want to buy a 'coffee-shop' or a 'shop-window' in the red-light area, no bank will finance you. You are forced to seek money from dodgy sources like organised crime. Whereas the coffee shops exist, they now have problems from the EU-wide no-smoking rules and their own suppliers are in a very difficult situation as although a shop may avoid prosecution, a wholesaler should be prosecuted even under Dutch law.

This is also about a property grab. Having hookers and dope cafes in the middle of town depressed property prices which have been elsewhere shooting through the roof.

The only reason for that, however, as well as for Amsterdam's troubles, is that even soft drugs like pot are illegal in 95% of the world, so people flock to the few places they are legal.

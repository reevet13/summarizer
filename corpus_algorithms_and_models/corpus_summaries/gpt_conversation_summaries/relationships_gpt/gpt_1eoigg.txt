I love my friends and family. I have what feels like a nigh-pathological inability to let others know about my failures. Senior year, my parents pressured me to enroll in their first choice college instead of mine. My first semester was a fiasco. This resulted in academic probation. Sophomore year, I backslid, and my grades were bad enough for the dean to put me on a mandatory semester of leave. I intercepted the letter that was meant to inform my parents of my suspension, and I led them to believe I was going back for my fourth semester. I bummed around that summer and, the next semester, I went to community college halfway in between my home-city and my college's city, trying to improve my GPA to get back into the college my parents thought I was attending. I got a 4.0, but my dean balked on some administrative matters and I was told I wouldn't be able to return until the next fall. So I orchestrated my most daring lie yet, which was that I was going to study abroad. I got great grades, and was accepted back to my original college. Halfway into the semester I came down with a bacterial infection that wrecked my life for a while. I slept twelve hours a day, and I ended up failing two classes. They were *insane* with anger, but I still covered up the truth. So I have been home this spring semester, and I've made excellent grades at my local college. I want to stay in my home-city, excel in classes, and be around people I love. I have been consumed by guilt about this for three years, and I just keep escalating my situation. **TL;DR: In order to avoid my parents' disappointment, I hid my failures in college for three years. I've finally started to get my life together this semester, but they are very close to finding out that I received low grades in three semesters of attendance, instead of decent grades in six semesters. I don't know how to salvage this situation. Ideally, I would keep them in the dark in order to not risk aggravating their health problems.**

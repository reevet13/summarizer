I think universal healthcare would be better implemented through public-private partnerships, as the French do it - you get the funding at the national level and dole it out per population to regions to administer.
You want the regions to be similar in size - you don't want California and Montana on the same administrative structure, you want interstate regional, like the circuit courts.
Below that, you (could) have private insuring companies competing relatively freely for the dollars based on how many they insure, and you give people the choice of who to use - whether that's tied to their employer, school, or just a regional company.
You also don't allow the insuring companies to cross regional boundaries, and you make sure to even the bureaucratic playing field so that the larger companies don't get a big advantage (try to make the administrative costs scale linearly, rather than logarithmically - paradoxically, you want O(n) for the number of insured, if at all possible, or else you end up with the insurance companies monopolizing a region - while this leads to higher absolute costs, it avoids the cost of a monopoly, which is much higher).
At the national level, you'd just have uniform taxation - to keep regions like the west coast from having better quality of service than the midwest - and likely a national health card.

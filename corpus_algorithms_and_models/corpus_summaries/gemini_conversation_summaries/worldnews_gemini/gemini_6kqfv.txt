Libertarians, take note:

Food supplies should not be controlled by folks who have an economic interest in keeping us dependent upon them. Having corn that cannot be replanted is only good for Monsanto, not good for consumers. Creating crops resistant to intense pesticides and herbicides so that you can use more and more chemicals on the field is great for the manufacturers of pesticides and herbicides -- which is why you see them directing research that way, instead of focusing on making more resistant plants. We end up with chemical-laden food that costs more than it needs to, because big agribusiness wants to keep farmers tethered to using their proprietary products.

It's not really much different from when mining companies used to pay miners in company scrip, which could only be used to buy things at the (overpriced) company store. Farmers today are barely getting by while big ag profits go to the companies that are screwing them over. And agribusiness doesn't just screw over farmers. The air you breathe, the water you drink, and the food you eat has all felt the impact of agribusinesses' "profit above all" strategy.
I'd rather have shady politicians than shady corporate overlords. I can exercise my right to vote politicians out of office. I have no similar means of control over the CEO of Monsanto, unless I'd like to stop eating entirely.

I believe in strong regulation of both the political and the corporate.
Your right to make money ends when, for instance, you allow agricultural chemicals to run off into the water where I wanted to fish or swim. That water doesn't belong to just one company. It belongs to all of us.

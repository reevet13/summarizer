However, the part about how we shouldn't ask athletes to give up their lifelong dreams is silly.

1. The idea that the *whole* *world* is somehow obligated to bend over backwards to accommodate such dreams, is ridiculous.

2. The people who came is second (and third and fourth ...) in the olympic try-outs had to give up their dreams.
Making the winners give up theirs, too, doesn't change much.

3. If someone's entire life is focussed on a 5-minute performance the summer they are 20, then they don't really have a life.
Perhaps they ought to get one.
Don't expect me to get too upset if their current plan falls through.

Don't get me wrong.
If someone can run very fast, or whatever, then I say good for them.
But I think it is nutty to say that one person's dreams of competing in a sporting event should be an important influence on national foreign policy.

(UPDATE: To clarify, I do *not* think that boycotting the Olympics is a good idea.
My point in this comment is that, of the three arguments the article advances to support this position [that a boycott is bad], the first two are sensible, but the third is silly.)

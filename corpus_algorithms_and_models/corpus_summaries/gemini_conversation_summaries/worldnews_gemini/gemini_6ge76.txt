It's the rich *countries* this article is talking about, not rich individuals.
Rich countries like the U.S., where in my state of Minnesota the government pays a subsidy for every gallon of corn ethanol produced, despite the booming growth in this industry (and hence the lack of a need for a subsidy).
This is in addition to the federal subsidy for corn ethanol.
What the rich countries of the world need to do is stop subsidizing farmers in general and ethanol in particular, immediately.
Let farmers grow their crops and let the market determine where the crops should go.
Ethanol subsidies are perverting the market to shunt corn to fuel, not to food, and that is the real travesty.
People who don't like biofuels love to say that they're causing the food crisis.
It's really not that simple, though.
First, the amount of grain that goes to biofuel is pretty minimal in proportion to the amount that goes to other "wastes" like the obscene amounts of high fructose corn syrup in foods and drinks, or feeding cattle because we can't get enough beef.
Second we have a grain surplus, and could easily feed the world's population with available food supplies if it were that easy.
The problem is distribution.
Transportation is prohibitively expensive, and that's one of the problems biofuels are trying to alleviate, albeit not very efficiently at the moment.
Blaming biofuels for world starvation is like blaming computer users for global warming.
Yes, we are to blame, but not nearly at the top of the list.
That's not to say biofuel is the fuel of the future, but oil isn't getting any cheaper either.

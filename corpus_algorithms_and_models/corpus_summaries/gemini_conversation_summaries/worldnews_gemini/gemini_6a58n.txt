Literature contains our cultural heritage.
You can learn a lot about a time just by reading books written then.
I once read William James's *Varieties of Religious Experience*, and found the book a bit dull except as an example of the political and religious climate of the late 19th century.

Without understanding the general culture of a time and region it is difficult to attack the more complex works of that time and region.
See how people misinterpret something as commonplace as the Bible since they lack a grounding in ancient Hebrew and Greek culture.
Philosophical works tend to draw allusions and such from the extant literature of their times.

Reading books from a given time also allows you to absorb history in a much richer way.
You are exposed to much more depth than the dry summary found in a history book, and are then more able to see the inconsistencies in such summaries.
Your mind is forced to work out contradictions between works, and generally just *think* while reading them.
All of this is very helpful toward improving your reasoning abilities.

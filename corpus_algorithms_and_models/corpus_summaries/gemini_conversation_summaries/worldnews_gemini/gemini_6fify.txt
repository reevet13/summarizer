We should stop describing ourselves as such.
It just plays into the hands of theists.
It makes us sound like some kind of rival cult that stands in need of justification (not that *religious* cults can provide justification for their beliefs).

An atheist is not a member of some kind of secular cult or club, he/she is just someone who refuses to accept non-empirical metaphysical claims, someone who refuses to make normative and substantive claims about superstitious belief.

Describing ourselves as atheist is bizarre.
I don't believe in unicorns either, or the reality of fairy stories, but I don't feel the need to use special terms to describe my non-belief in such things!

Why should I need to indicate my *opting out* of superstition?
Superstition is *opt-in*!
It is those who hold belief who are obliged to provide grounds for their belief.

If we must refer to our non-belief, then instead of "atheist", we should describe ourselves as "*non-superstitious*" or (if you have no truck with religious belief but are superstitious in other ways) "*non-metaphysicalists*", or some other term which should ideally be both awkward to use in ordinary conversation and that highlights the heart of the matter, namely that theists believe in something for which no empirical grounds can be given.

Theism is not a *norm* from which we are obliged to indicate our having opted-out!

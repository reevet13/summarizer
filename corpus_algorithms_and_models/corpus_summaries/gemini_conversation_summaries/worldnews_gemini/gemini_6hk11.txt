It's a bit convoluted.
We don't vote for the executive (the Prime Minister and his Cabinet) directly in the U.K.; we vote for the legislature, and whatever party gets the most seats in Parliament forms the executive with its leader in the House of Commons taking the post of Prime Minister.
In the case of Gordon Brown, Tony Blair resigned, and Gordon Brown won the Labour leadership election (only members of the Labour party could take part in that) and so, as leader of the Labour Party in the House of Commons, became Prime Minister.
As there is no election for Prime Minister, PMs don't have terms.
We have Parliamentatry General Elections which have to be called every 5 years or less, but within that time frame the executive can choose when exactly to do it.

There are horses that are hauled to Canada and Mexico for slaughter.
There are horse feedlots in Montana, where skinny horses are fed up just south of the Canadian border, so they are only a short shipping distance from the plant.
But I still support a no slaughter policy, because as someone who was active in the horse trade, I know that it wasn't the old horses or the crippled horses that mostly went for slaughter.
Most of the horses that went for slaughter did so - because they were far more valuable by the pound than they were as back yard horses.
I would guess that only one tenth of the horses previously shipped from this territory are still going for slaughter.
Currently, many fat horses only bring around 500 in the spring, and 300 in the fall.
But to have to go through the cost of health certificates, moving the horses up north for slaughter has saved many horses' lives.
It isn't as though Canada will just slaughter an unlimited number of horses, or everything everyone can get up to them.
The whole thing where they are saying that crippled horses go for slaughter is for the most part myth, though.
Roping horses, 4-h horses, people's personal pets, just every cared for horse - the fat ones were the ones that were most prized for meat.
These horses wouldn't be available at that kind of price if horses were still going to slaughter in large numbers.

Early last week Business Day published a letter by Essop Pahad's special advisor, Tony Heard in which Heard spoke of his "unshakeable belief that mediator Thabo Mbeki has, more than anyone else, pushed Zimbabwe, be it quietly, towards a return from deepening hell."
A couple of days later Mbeki gave an interview to Al Jazeera in which he said that the Mugabe regime (or rather, the "people of Zimbabwe") needed to be given "space".
He added that there was no need for the United Nations to observe the run-off of Zimbabwe's presidential poll.
This statement flies in the face of the request by MDC leader, Morgan Tsvangirai, for the poll to be supervised by international observers led by the UN.
On Wednesday last week (May 7) that same newspaper published a column by Thomas L. Friedman which presented a somewhat less flattering view of our president.
The only one who rivals him is his neighbour and chief enabler and protector, South Africa's president, Thabo Mbeki....
Last week the lawyers for the suspended National Director of Public Prosecutions, Vusi Pikoli, documented - in great detail - how he had been defenestrated for refusing a government order to desist from executing an arrest and search warrant against National Police Commissioner, Jackie Selebi.
He was told to resign, and after his refusal to do so he was suspended.
After it emerged that a warrant had been issued for Selebi's arrest shortly before Pikoli's suspension government said that these two matters were in no way related.

It looks like Continentals trying to "game the system" in using the 757 for it's near-maximum range flight profile.
This "built-in" issue means that the crew can reliably call "Minimum fuel" and thus get on the ground before other equally-deserving airliners.
"Several employee bulletins dug up by the FAA show that Continental management was concerned by the number of fuel stops pilots were making on international flights into Newark, at one point going so far as to connect the costs of unnecessary refueling with profit sharing and pension funding."
They never fill the tanks completely.
Each flight has just enough fuel to get there plus a safety margin.
&gt;FAA found all of them in compliance with this rule -- on average they had 64 minutes of fuel remaining.
Well, the point of using the 757's is to say, "Look, we just flew 3300 miles.  This plane's max range is 3900.  We need to get on the ground."
By using a plane that approaches its limits in range, they have a more plausible excuse for getting on the ground faster and using less fuel.
"Minimum fuel" doesn't necessarily get you on the ground faster (only "emergency fuel" will do that.) And both are investigated and tracked by the FAA.
That is incentive enough for Continental to put new aircraft on that route (which are on-order, deliveries should start in 2009.)

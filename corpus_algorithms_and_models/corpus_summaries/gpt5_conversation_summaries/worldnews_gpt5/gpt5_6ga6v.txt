I had read and lost a good report that boiled down to 3-4% of the food price increase is due to biofuels.
Food demand is very inelastic.
However biofuels have a horrible EROI.
Of course global food shortages are linked to biofuel use - everything on earth is somehow linked.
But that does not mean that biofuels are responsible for the current rises in food prices.
1 - Climate events (unusual harmful weather) severely reduced grain harvests in Australia and North America.
2 - Rice and grain consumption in China, India and much of the 3rd world has steadily increased for years, and the rate of increase is now accelerating far faster than agricultural productivity.
3 - Unstable oil market (Iraq) simultaneous with greatly increased demand, especially from growing India and China, increased oil prices, which increased the price of everything from fuel for production and transport of food to chemical fertilizers.
4 - Diversion of previously food producing lands into inefficient but profitable biofuels reduced food supply.
I don't know...economists say biofuels should only contribute 3-4.5% increase in food costs.
Algae does not compete for cropland.
It produces 30x more energy per acre than terrestrial crops.
Remember that food demand is pretty inelastic.

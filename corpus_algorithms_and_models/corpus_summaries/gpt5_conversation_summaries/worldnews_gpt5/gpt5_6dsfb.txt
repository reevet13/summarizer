A human right isn't something the government provides, its something you are born with -- the government can't take away.
Let it be said any society should provide safe drinking water, and I'll agree.
But, water isn't a "right".
Basically Canada's fear is that with such a large part of the world's fresh water supply being present in Canada, saying that it is a basic right removes any control Canada has on that supply.
A right is something fundamental to humanity, but doesn't necessarily mean it's something a government has to "provide".
And there are few things more fundamental to staying alive than having water.
So it's a basic human right here already.
The US is not required to send all of its food to starving people, so why would Canada have to send its water?
Making drinking water a basic human right is a very ugly grey area that is hard to enforce.
But it conflicts with a nations's right to sovereignty.
Water is a special resource that cannot be made into property.
Some rivers flow through multiple countries.
On principle I agree with access to water as a human right, but I think the details of the consequences of what such a statement means need to be spelled out much further before I'd agree to it.

This article was published on Thursday July 17 2003.
Blair earned a lot of hatred for his blind following of the US and the public no longer see many benefits of the "special relationship" we have.
In fact he won three elections in a row, was unprecedently popular and successful for a Labour leader, and now he's stepped down Labour are polling worse than they ever have in their history.
There's a lot of "voluntary" collaboration going on, on all levels.
He endured massive criticism for his following of the US and this ended the days of him being incredibly popular.
Still, Britain is nowhere near as independent or powerful as we all like to believe.
And yet tens of thousands of American troops, missle bases, and aircraft are still camped all over Europe - they haven't been "protecting" us from the Russians for 16 years... so why are they still here?
Britain isn't the only nation that has an irritatingly unequal relationship with the US, most of the rest of Europe is in the same boat.. for now.
The British sold their sovereignty to the US out of desperation during WWII.
Thus, the US didnt forcefully take their sovereignty, the British just gave it away so that they wouldn't become part of Germany during WWII.
Gordon Brown was an integral part of Blair's government, so he is seen as simply an extension of the Blair era.

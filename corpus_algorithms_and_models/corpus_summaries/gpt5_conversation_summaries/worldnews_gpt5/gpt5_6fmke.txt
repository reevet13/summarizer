Now if they were *self-sufficient*, or financed completely by their own church, then there might be an argument to be had here.  As long as they accept public funds, they should have to conform to public standards.
We focus more on non-discrimination and I would venture to say that in this case they clearly violate those laws.
The school can enact its teaching by having religious education as a compulsary unit, whereas public schools cannot.
It does not give them a free license to use their teaching to discriminate against people who do not conform to their religion.
People send their kids to private schools in Australia to get a better education, not so they get taught religion.
